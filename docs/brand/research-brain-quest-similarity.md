# Research: BRUSH QUEST vs. BRAIN QUEST trademark distinctness

**Researched:** 2026-04-28 (background agent, deep TTAB + DuPont analysis)

## TL;DR

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| P(clean USPTO grant for "BRUSH QUEST" word mark in 9 + 41) | **~25-35%** |
| P(Workman Publishing files opposition if we survive examination) | **~50-70%** |
| P(survive opposition on the merits, DIY) | **~20-35%** |
| **Cumulative P(clean word-mark registration)** | **~15-25%** |

**Recommendation: File a STYLIZED/DESIGN MARK (logo + wordmark composite), NOT the word mark.**
- Drops Office Action risk to ~10-15%
- Gives defensive moat against direct copycats
- Avoids head-on conflict with Workman's word-mark family
- DIY-feasible

The user's intuition that "BRAIN and BRUSH are different first words" is linguistically correct but NOT how examiners and the TTAB analyze "[noun] QUEST" marks in the kids-education space.

## 1. TTAB Precedent — Workman has actively opposed similar marks

| Case | Mark opposed | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Opposition 91196190** (2010) | BRAIN TWIST | SUSTAINED 2010-12-07. Application abandoned. |
| **Opposition 91113716** (1999) | BRAINQUEST (BrainQuest Foundation) | SUSTAINED 2000-11-21. Default judgment. |

**Interpretation:** Workman has a documented pattern of opposing BRAIN-prefix and "BRAIN [X]" marks. BRAIN TWIST is the more telling precedent — different second word but Workman framed BRAIN as the dominant element and won. BRUSH QUEST inverts this (same second word, different first), but examiners give comparable weight to either pattern.

No precedential decision was issued in either case (both ended in defaults), so neither is binding, but they signal Workman's enforcement posture.

## 2. Workman Publishing enforcement profile

- Confirmed: at least 2 BRAIN QUEST oppositions filed, both won by default
- Hachette (Workman's parent since 2021) public statement: "will aggressively enforce our Trademark rights to the fullest extent of the law, including the seeking of criminal prosecution"
- Brain Quest is a 30+ year brand with 56M+ copies in print, "America's #1 educational bestseller" — fame is substantial under DuPont factor 5
- Workman is an **opposer, not a litigator** — they file TTAB oppositions, not federal suits
- Cost to defend a TTAB opposition: $10-30K. Even poorly-resourced applicants typically abandon rather than fight

## 3. DuPont Factor Analysis (honest, not optimistic)

| DuPont Factor | BRUSH QUEST vs BRAIN QUEST | Net |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Similarity of marks | Same syllable count (1+1), same suffix QUEST, both first words start "BR" + 1-syllable noun, 6/10 letters identical, same consonant onset, different vowels. Meaning differs (cleaning tool vs. organ). | Slightly favors Workman |
| 2. Similarity of goods/services | Both are kids' interactive educational entertainment. Brain Quest registered in classes 9/38/41 for game software, electronic game programs, video games. Brush Quest = same classes. | **Strongly favors Workman** |
| 3. Channels of trade | App stores, parent-targeted marketing, kids-education category. Overlapping retail (Amazon, parent blogs, kids-app review sites). | Favors Workman |
| 4. Sophistication of buyers | Buyer = parent paying ~$0-5 for kids' app. Low cost, low scrutiny, impulse. Low buyer care = examiners weight confusion higher. | **Strongly favors Workman** |
| 5. Strength/fame of senior mark | 30+ years use, 56M+ copies, NYT bestseller, "America's #1 educational bestseller" positioning. Likely entitled to broad protection. | **Strongly favors Workman** |
| 6. Number of similar marks on similar goods | "[X] QUEST" exists (BUBBLE SHOOTER QUEST, 3RD EYE QUEST, etc.) but none target kids-edu-flashcard niche. Crowded-field argument is weaker than it looks. | Mildly favors Brush Quest |
| 13. Bad faith / intent | Clean record. No copying intent, no piggyback marketing. | Favors Brush Quest |

**Net:** 5 factors favor Workman (including the heaviest-weighted 1, 2, 5), 2 mildly favor Brush Quest. Examiner more likely than not to issue §2(d) Office Action.

## 4. Coexistence examples don't help us

The "[X] QUEST" coexisting marks all sit in adjacent but non-identical product spaces:

- **BUBBLE SHOOTER QUEST** — match-3 game, general audience, not kids' education
- **3RD EYE QUEST** — physical card games for adults, class 28
- **BLACK BETTERMENT QUEST** — classes 35/42, consulting services
- **DRAGON QUEST** — RPGs ≠ educational flashcards. Notably, Dragon Quest was renamed "Dragon Warrior" in early US releases to avoid a separate trademark conflict — illustrating even big publishers concede when goods overlap

**None share BRUSH QUEST's profile** (kids under 8, educational, parents are buyers, mobile-app channel). Crowded-field argument exists but is thin in our specific segment.

## 5. Recommendation (the synthesis)

1. **Don't file the word mark "BRUSH QUEST" alone.** Combined examination + opposition risk too high; contested TTAB defense costs $10-30K — outsized for a solo founder.
2. **File a stylized/design mark** (BRUSH QUEST logo with the toothbrush + space ranger visual). Stylization shifts factor 1 sharply in our favor — Brain Quest's logo is question-mark-and-typography with no toothbrush/space iconography. Office Action risk drops to ~10-15%; opposition risk drops because the visual element is genuinely distinct.
3. **Defer the word mark filing** until either (a) Brush Quest reaches commercial scale that justifies legal counsel, or (b) a rebrand to a non-"QUEST" mark is on the table.
4. **Consider rebrand only if** Workman sends a C&D over the actual app launch (separate from trademark filing).
